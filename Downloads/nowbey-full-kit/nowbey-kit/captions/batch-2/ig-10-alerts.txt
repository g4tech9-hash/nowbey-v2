Two things your family needs to know, and only one of them is an app. 🌧️

Official alerts come from officials. A MARCO Alert — The Bahamas' missing-child alert, issued by the Royal Bahamas Police Force — reaches your phone through your carrier, the way it always has. So do the hurricane warnings from NEMA and the Met Office. We are not going to pretend a community map replaces any of that. In an emergency, call 911 / 919.

Here is the part those alerts do not cover: whether your lane is passable at 7:40 this morning. Whether the line is down on your street. Whether the water has gone off at your end of the road. That knowledge lives with neighbours — so we gave it somewhere to live.

Set up free email alerts. Pick your island, then pick what you actually want to hear about: flooding, road hazards, power. One profile per email address. No spam.

And the promise we would rather keep than monetise: storm warnings are always sent to everyone, on every plan, for free. That is not a growth tactic. That is just the right way round.

Being straight with you — email is the channel that works today. Text-message alerts and iPhone notifications are not built yet, and we will say so here when they are.
